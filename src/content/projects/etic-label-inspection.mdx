## Problem
Checking labels on a production line by eye is slow and error-prone. The goal was a prototype that reads and checks label content automatically from camera images.

## Context
Built as a prototype during a 1-month Computer Vision summer internship at ETIC Europe (industrial quality control).

## Architecture
- **Preprocessing**: camera calibration, distortion correction, adaptive thresholding, denoising and orientation correction.
- **Text detection**: EAST.
- **Text reading**: OCR with Tesseract.
- **QR codes**: decoding with OpenCV.
- **Interface**: C# / .NET 8 WinForms desktop app to follow inspection results.

## Technology
| Technology | Role |
|---|---|
| OpenCV / OpenCvSharp4 | Image preprocessing, calibration, QR reading |
| EAST | Text region detection |
| Tesseract | OCR on detected regions |
| C# / .NET 8 (WinForms) | Desktop interface |

## Pipeline
1. **Acquisition**: image from the camera.
2. **Calibration & correction**: lens distortion and orientation corrected.
3. **Preprocessing**: adaptive thresholding and denoising to handle varying angles and lighting.
4. **Detection**: EAST locates text regions.
5. **Reading**: Tesseract extracts text; QR codes are decoded.
6. **Display**: results shown in the desktop interface.

## Challenges
Labels are seen under **varying angles and lighting**. Calibration, distortion correction and adaptive preprocessing were the main levers to make text detection and OCR more stable.

## What I learned
Computer Vision on real industrial images is very different from clean academic datasets: lighting, angle and camera geometry matter as much as the model.
